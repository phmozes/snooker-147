Player's view camera, round 2: the random draw of 4 cameras

Video clips only, the game is not changed. Your choice: for each shot one camera is drawn at random, equal chance (1 in 4): TODAY (57°), V1, V2, V3. All clips come from the real game (developer version, same code as the test build), 390×844, 30 frames a second, in one recorded round (reds15-v3-3191475780, 15 reds). Each step is filmed as it is really shot inside the round, so the top bar, the ladder and the step count are the real ones of that shot. A clip starts at the SHOOT press (the cue's 0.25 s stroke included) and ends 0.5 s after SHOOT comes back. In every V clip the camera goes back to 57° before SHOOT comes back.

The fixed rules inside V1, V2 and V3 (used in every V clip below except the ones marked "as before")

  • Rise path (V3): the point the camera looks at moves in a straight line, and the camera's angle above the cloth, its direction round the table and its distance are blended separately. The angle only grows from 5° to 57°, so the path never looks straight down (measured: highest angle in every fixed clip 57.0°).
  • Over the cue: the camera always stands at least 9 cm above the cue's centre line at its own spot. A cue raised over a ball or a cushion lifts the camera (H2, H3).
  • Over the rail: if the camera spot is outside the cloth, it stays at least 10 cm above the rail top.
  • Balls in the way: a ball between the camera and the cue ball (or within 10 cm of the camera) is drawn see-through (22%) while the camera holds, and solid again when the camera goes back to 57°.
  • Full cue: today the cue is shortened so that, from 57°, it never covers the object ball or its pocket. From behind the cue ball that rule is not needed, so the V cameras show the whole cue.
  • All of these depend only on the layout and the planned cue direction, never on the outcome. No V camera ever falls back to TODAY.

1 · V3 rise fixed: the 3 shots of round 1

The old V3 (straight blend of position and look-at point) passes through a close top-down view of the cloth about 0.5 s after the hit; the new one rises along an arc. Shot 1 = straight red into a corner (step 7), shot 2 = thin cut on the brown (step 33), shot 3 = long red (step 27).

2 · Back to 57° after the shot, before SHOOT comes back

R1 = instant cut back to 57° the moment the balls stop. R2 = a 0.4 s glide back, and SHOOT waits for it. Extra seconds per shot: R1 0 s; R2 +0.4 s on every V1 and V2 shot (measured: SHOOT came back 0.43 s later for V1 and 0.43 s later for V2; 0.4 s plus up to one frame). V3 is already at 57° when the balls stop (its rise ends 1.0 s after the hit), so R1 and R2 look the same there and need 0 s in the game. In this mockup the V3 R2 clip still waited the 0.4 s (SHOOT 0.43 s later); the game would skip that wait when the camera is already at 57°. Only a shot whose balls stop less than 1.0 s after the hit would need the cut or the glide on V3.

3 · Pocket camera with the new draw (proposal, not built)

Only one special camera per shot. The camera draw (1 in 4 each) is made from a visual-only seeded stream before the shot, so it says nothing about the outcome. Today's pocket cut: 1 in 6 of shots from step 2 (never step 1 or the last ball), same for a pot and a miss; it adds about 1.9 s per cut (NOW.md: +0.32 s per shot at 1 in 6). PC: the pocket cut is equal for pot and miss (as today), so the rest, (1 - 1/6) / 4 for each camera, is equal too.
  • Keeping 1 in 6 pocket cuts only on TODAY shots is not possible: it needs 4x the chances, and the miss chances (0.51-0.75 now) cannot go above 100%. The most is PB (5.6 per 100).
  • PA is the simplest to check. PC keeps as many pocket cuts as today, but a V camera is then used only on shots without a pocket cut, so V is not "drawn first" any more (the rates still stay equal).
